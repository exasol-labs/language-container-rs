## Premortem

Three failure stories bounded to what the round-1 fixes themselves could cause.

1. **A never-exercised assertion ships.** `language_definitions_declares_schema_version_2` is written as `jq '.schema_version'` without `-e`, or compares a number against the string `"2"`, so it passes on any value. No negative fixture targets it, the conforming case is green, and the four `PASS:` lines the § Manual Testing row expects all appear. A later DB bump to `schema_version: 3` is transcribed into `build_info/language_definitions.json`, the guardrail stays silent, and the tarball ships a version the parser rejects — the same class of blind assertion this plan exists to remove. → Requirement Quality A1.
2. **The fixture base rots.** Task 2 and task 3 sit in the same parallel group and independently transcribe the conforming document from the § Migration table; nothing compares the two. A year later a new required field enters the contract. `conforming.json` reds until someone updates it, but the five negatives are not updated, so each now fails for two reasons at once. The one-defect-per-fixture property that was the entire purpose of task 2 is silently gone, and the next real defect in one of those five fields ships green. → Design Depth A2.
3. **Group B lands half-applied.** The implementer of task 5 edits `.github/workflows/ci.yml` and verifies the new step locally before the concurrently-running task 2 has written the driver. `bash dist/tests/language_definitions_fixtures_test.sh` reports `No such file or directory`, and the implementer "fixes" the step by dropping the driver invocation. The fixture suite exists, is committed, and never runs in CI. → Task Breakdown A3.
